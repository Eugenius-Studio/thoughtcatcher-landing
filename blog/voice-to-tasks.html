Feature guide

Turn a spoken brain dump into tasks with Blurts

Capture several intentions in one short recording, then review clean tasks with useful details in Blurts. Learn the flow and its limits.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

Get the intentions out before they disappear

A thought rarely arrives as a perfectly organized checklist. You remember a work follow-up, then an errand, then a conversation you need to have. Blurts lets you speak those intentions in one short recording and extracts actionable items into separate tasks. You can keep your attention on what you mean to do rather than composing a polished note first.

The current mobile capture flow records audio, processes it online, and saves the extracted tasks before showing the results. The results screen is a summary of what has been captured. Editing happens in your task list afterward. This distinction matters: you do not need to select each generated task and press a second save button to create it.

Make your first capture

Open Blurts and tap the recording control. Allow microphone access when your phone asks. Speak the actions you want to remember, then stop. A recording can run for up to two minutes; the app stops at that limit. Give a short recording enough time to contain clear speech rather than immediately tapping start and stop.

Wait for processing and inspect the resulting tasks. Blurts can add a concise title, supporting description, task type, area, priority, and a due date when you mention one. These are interpretations of your words, so check the details that change what you will do. If you connected another destination, distinguish the saved tasks from the separate delivery result.

  • Start with a few ordinary actions rather than a long meeting.
  • Name the action explicitly: “ask for the quote” is clearer than “the quote.”
  • Open the task list to correct wording, timing, or classification.

An example worth trying

You might say: “Send Lena the revised outline tomorrow, pick up printer paper, and check whether the meeting room is available.” The intended outcome is three distinct commitments. Check that the room item remains a request to check availability, rather than an instruction to book it. This example illustrates what to inspect; it is not a guarantee of identical output every time.

You do not have to assign every item a date. An undated task is often more accurate than a fabricated deadline. If your thought is only an idea, say what decision or next action you intend to make. AI extraction can interpret vague remarks as tasks, so remove an item that does not represent a commitment you want to keep.

What to do when capture is interrupted

Task extraction needs an internet connection. Recording and online processing are different stages, and a network error is not proof that the result reached the task list. Check the list before repeating a capture so you do not unintentionally create duplicates. The current flow is designed to survive ordinary backgrounding, but a fully terminated app can interrupt an in-flight operation.

If the app reports no speech, try a short clear phrase and check microphone permission. If the result is saved but an integration fails, address that destination connection rather than re-recording the entire thought. Capture limits follow your account’s current plan; use the in-app offer and allowance as the source for entitlement details.

Common questions

Does one recording create only one task?

No. The extraction flow can create several tasks from a single recording when your speech contains several actionable items.

Can I edit before saving?

The current flow saves extracted tasks before presenting its results. Open the task list and use Edit afterward to correct them.

Does voice capture work entirely offline?

No. Audio is processed online to produce tasks. Previously cached tasks can remain visible, but that is different from offline extraction.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
