Feature guide

Capture and review Blurts tasks from home-screen widgets

Use Blurts Record and task-list widgets for quicker access, understand the four-task snapshot, and know which items the widget leaves out.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

Put the entrance to your list where you notice it

Blurts includes home-screen widgets for starting a capture and glancing at tasks. A widget can remove the step of finding the app icon when an intention arrives, and a task snapshot can keep a few upcoming items visible between app visits. The widget is an entrance to the workflow, not a separate task manager.

The current iPhone widgets include a small Blurts Record widget and a medium Blurts Tasks widget. Android also includes recording and task-list widget components. Their layouts and the system’s widget picker differ by platform, but both are designed around quick access to the same mobile app.

Add a widget and confirm the shortcut

Use your phone’s home-screen widget picker and find Blurts. Add the recording widget where it is easy to reach, or choose the task-list widget when a visible reminder of upcoming work is more useful. Open Blurts at least once and ensure the tasks you expect are available in the app.

Tap the recording shortcut to open the capture flow. Microphone permission and the normal recording requirements still apply. The task-list surface opens the task list, and its recording affordance can start a new capture. You review and manage the result inside Blurts rather than editing all task fields on the widget itself.

  • Choose the recording widget for quick entry.
  • Choose the task-list widget for a small upcoming-work snapshot.
  • Use the app for editing, completion, and a full review.

Understand the four-task snapshot

The widget receives up to four pending, non-archived tasks from the local task store. Items with a due date before today are excluded. Dated tasks are ordered ahead of undated ones, with due date and priority used in the selection. As a result, the widget is not a complete display of everything you still owe.

This is especially important when your list contains overdue work. The main app can show those items under “Still on your list,” while the widget deliberately omits them. An empty or short widget therefore should not be interpreted as proof that every commitment is complete. Open the full list when making a plan.

A useful everyday setup

Suppose you often remember an errand while switching between messages and your calendar. Put the capture widget on that same home screen. Tap it, record the action, and check the saved result. You have made the capture route easier to find without creating a different inbox.

If your aim is reviewing upcoming work, place the task-list widget where you see it before opening a distracting app. Treat the visible items as a prompt to choose or inspect a task. If the item you expected is absent, check whether it is completed, archived, overdue, or simply outside the four-item snapshot.

When the widget looks out of date

Widget content is a snapshot, and the operating system controls when it redraws. Open Blurts, let the local list update, and return to the home screen before concluding that data is missing. A widget refresh is not the same as a live connection to every integrated service.

If a recording tap does not start the expected flow, open the app directly and check microphone access. Do not use widget behavior to infer that audio has already been captured or processed. The saved task in the app is the result to verify, and online extraction is still required after recording.

Common questions

Can I see every task in the widget?

No. The current snapshot contains at most four eligible pending tasks and excludes overdue and archived items.

Can I edit a task directly in the widget?

The reviewed widgets link into the app. Use the task list and editor for changes.

Does a widget make capture work offline?

No. It gives you a quicker entrance to the recording flow. The final speech-to-task processing still needs a connection.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
