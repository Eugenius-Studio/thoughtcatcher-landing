Feature guide

Add task dates and times by speaking in Blurts

Capture dates such as tomorrow or next week, add a clock time, and understand how Blurts groups the resulting tasks without creating calendar events.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

Keep timing attached to the right action

A useful task often needs more than a title. Blurts can interpret a date or time you mention while recording and attach it to the relevant task. If you do not mention a date, the extraction is instructed to leave the task undated. This helps preserve the difference between a real deadline and something you simply want to remember.

Date interpretation uses the current local date and time sent by the mobile app. Relative week phrases also use your start-of-week preference. The result can be an all-day date or a date with a clock time. It remains a task due date; this feature does not create a calendar invitation or reserve time with other people.

Speak the action and its timing together

Start a recording and say what needs doing, followed by when it matters. For example, “Call the supplier tomorrow at 3pm, and compare the two quotes next week.” Stop, allow processing, and check that the two different timings are attached to the correct tasks. A date is useful only if it belongs to the intended action.

Open Edit on a task to correct the date or add a time using the date and time controls. A time requires a date. You can remove the time to make the task all-day, or clear the date when no deadline is appropriate. Review the displayed result after saving rather than assuming every relative phrase has one obvious interpretation.

  • Use an explicit date when a deadline must be unambiguous.
  • Use a clock time when “morning” or “later” would be too vague.
  • Set your preferred week start before relying on “next week.”

How relative phrases are handled

The current extraction rules resolve “tomorrow” from today and “next week” to the first day of the next week according to the stored week-start preference. A named weekday is interpreted as its next occurrence. A time without a date is intended to mean today, or tomorrow when that time has already passed.

These rules help with ordinary capture but do not replace review. “Next Friday” can carry different meanings in different conversations. If an event has a fixed external deadline, use its actual date and verify it. The app can reject malformed calendar values, but a valid date can still be the wrong interpretation of your intention.

What appears in the list and connected tools

Blurts uses due dates to organize tasks into date groups and show times on timed items. Tasks with earlier dates can appear under “Still on your list.” You can adjust priority separately; a clock time does not by itself tell the app how important the task is compared with other work.

Connected services have their own date semantics. In particular, a task sent to Google Tasks is not a Google Calendar event, and you should not assume that every destination preserves a due-time value identically. Likewise, the Blurts daily reminder is a separate morning notification, not an alarm scheduled for every spoken task time.

Common questions

Does every task get a due date?

No. Dates are optional and the extraction is instructed to add one only when timing is mentioned.

Does saying 3pm create a 3pm notification?

A task time and a notification are separate features. The reviewed notification service provides a daily morning reminder; it is not a per-task alarm scheduler.

Why should I set the first day of my week?

That preference affects week grouping and how relative phrases such as “next week” are resolved. Review an exact date whenever the distinction matters.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
