Feature guide

See live words while recording in Blurts

Understand Blurts’ live transcription preview, device requirements, fallback behavior, and why the final tasks can differ from the live words.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

See feedback while you speak

Live transcription gives you visual feedback during a recording. When your device supports it, words appear as you talk, helping you see that the app is receiving speech. This can be useful when you are capturing several thoughts and want a quick cue that the recording is still active.

The live words are a preview, not the final task extraction. Blurts processes the saved audio separately after you stop. The resulting tasks may be shorter, split into separate actions, or worded differently from the live transcript. Review the task list as the output you will use rather than treating the live display as the saved task record.

Use it as part of an ordinary capture

Start recording from Blurts and allow the permissions requested by your device. Where on-device speech recognition is available, the preview runs alongside the audio recording. Speak naturally and stop when you have captured the actions. You do not need to copy the live text or send it through a second entry field.

If live words are unavailable, the recording flow can continue with a recording indicator. Availability depends on your device’s speech-recognition support and permissions. The current implementation intentionally disables this preview in the iOS simulator, so an absence of live text there is not evidence that audio capture is broken.

  • Watch the recording state and timer as well as the words.
  • Wait for final processing after stopping.
  • Edit the resulting task if the action or a name needs correction.

A useful example

Suppose you are listing three small follow-ups and briefly pause to remember the last one. The live display helps you see the words recognized so far while the recording continues. You can finish the thought without opening a notes app or trying to memorize the exact sentence you already spoke.

After processing, compare the tasks with your intended commitments. A preview containing your whole spoken sentence may become several task titles. That is expected when the sentence contains several actions. Conversely, seeing words appear does not mean every detail has been interpreted correctly; verify dates, conditions, and who needs to do what in the final output.

When the preview is blank or stops updating

First check whether audio recording is still active. The app is designed to let audio continue if live recognition is unavailable or encounters repeated errors. You can stop normally and inspect the final result. Do not repeatedly restart solely because the preview is blank; that can fragment one useful capture into several partial recordings.

On a physical device, check the permissions and speech support available to the app. A network connection is still required for final task extraction even though the preview requests on-device recognition. The existence of local preview text does not make the complete workflow offline, and it does not mean the final processing has already saved a task.

Common questions

Is the live transcript the text used to create tasks?

No. The live text provides feedback. Blurts separately transcribes the recorded audio for the final extraction.

Can I capture without live words appearing?

Yes. The implementation lets audio recording continue when live recognition is unavailable. Check the recording state, then inspect the final result.

Why is there no live preview in the simulator?

The current app disables this component in the iOS simulator because the native speech engine is unsuitable there. A genuine simulator screenshot should show the fallback state, not invented live text.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
