Blurts vs Microsoft To Do: spoken capture alongside your workday

Compare Blurts and Microsoft To Do for voice capture, My Day, Outlook follow-ups, account requirements, and keeping commitments in one place.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 4 min read
Decision Blurts Microsoft To Do
Primary use Capture scattered intentions by voice Daily lists, My Day and Microsoft task workflow
Microsoft destination Microsoft Planner plan and bucket Microsoft To Do is a different destination
Handoff No direct Microsoft To Do connector verified Do not assume Planner delivery equals To Do sync

Capture and daily review solve different parts of the problem

Microsoft To Do is a strong starting point when your tasks belong beside Outlook and your day begins with a deliberate list of priorities. Blurts focuses on an earlier moment: taking spoken thoughts and turning them into clean tasks. If To Do already works as your review system, the practical question is whether a different capture route helps you feed it without extra maintenance.

That makes this less of an automatic replacement decision than a workflow decision. You may need a better way to record thoughts without needing a new place to organize your day. Alternatively, the native tools you already use may be sufficient. Judge both choices by whether commitments arrive in the right place with enough context to act.

Microsoft To Do’s strengths are concrete

Microsoft’s product overview describes a free task app with list sharing, reminders, cross-device access, and Outlook tasks integration. My Day lets you choose tasks for today, supported by suggestions. The wider task experience includes details such as steps, repeat settings, notes, and files.

Flagged Outlook email is especially relevant for work follow-ups. For supported Microsoft-hosted accounts, flagged messages can appear in To Do with a route back to the email. Microsoft specifies limitations, including primary-mailbox behavior and the absence of support for shared-mailbox flags. Those details matter more than a generic claim that an app “integrates with Outlook.”

Voice claims need to be specific

A phone’s keyboard dictation, an operating-system assistant, and an app that separates a ramble into tasks are different capture paths. Compare the one you actually use. Microsoft still publishes Siri setup articles explicitly titled for older iOS versions. Those articles should not be read as a guarantee that every current iPhone configuration offers the same connection.

Blurts is designed around speaking a ramble and receiving clean tasks. The question for a Microsoft To Do user is how that capture experience fits the account and lists already used for work. The Microsoft integration in Blurts targets Microsoft Planner, using a selected plan and bucket. It is not a direct Microsoft To Do connector. Blurts can deliver captured work to that Planner destination, but later Blurts edits, completion, and deletion are not promised to update the delivered Planner task. If your requirement is personal To Do list capture or Outlook flagged-email processing, verify that workflow separately. The Planner integration guide explains account access, destination selection, and the delivery boundary.

Test a follow-up that starts away from Outlook

Suppose a colleague stops you after a meeting. You agree to check a delivery date, send a revised document after approval, and ask facilities about a room. None of those commitments has reached your inbox yet. Capture the thoughts while the details are fresh, then inspect whether the three actions remain distinct and the approval condition is still clear.

Continue the test the next morning. Can you find the commitments in the place where you choose your day’s work? Can you distinguish the task to check a delivery date from a task to confirm it? Can you add the right item to today’s plan without copying the entire note? These checks reveal whether the workflow helps beyond the moment of speaking.

  • Use the same Microsoft account you depend on during the workday.
  • Check the actual destination list and the task’s details after capture.
  • Verify what happens when you edit or complete a task; do not assume synchronization in both directions.

Keep the strengths of your current system

If most tasks originate as emails, the native Outlook-to-To Do path may already be the least work. Re-speaking an email can discard context or introduce a new interpretation step. Use a voice workflow for the moments it improves, rather than forcing every task through it simply because the capability is available.

If thoughts routinely arrive between meetings or away from your desk, a focused capture app may fill a gap. Test that gap with new tasks while leaving your established lists intact. You do not need to migrate a backlog, recreate recurring tasks, or change a shared household list to learn whether capturing a spoken set of follow-ups is useful.

Cost and connection details should decide the next step

Microsoft markets To Do as free. A separate paid capture app therefore needs to justify its incremental cost for your routine. Compare the current Blurts offer with the effort you actually save, including review and transfer. Do not count a successful transcription as a saved task until the result is somewhere you will use.

For a combined workflow, confirm the exact supported Microsoft destination and account requirements in Blurts. A Microsoft sign-in option alone does not prove support for every Microsoft service, list type, or synchronization behavior. Keep the definition of success simple: the intended task appears once, in the right place, with the details you need. Choose the workflow that meets that standard consistently in your trial.

Common questions

Is Microsoft To Do free?

Microsoft’s product page describes To Do as free. Separate services, account features, or a third-party capture subscription should be considered on their own terms.

Does Blurts replace My Day or Outlook?

Evaluate Blurts for the capabilities verified in its current app. A capture workflow can complement daily planning without replacing Outlook or every Microsoft To Do feature.

Can I assume a Microsoft integration has two-way sync?

No. Verify the destination, account, supported fields, and edit or completion behavior. Creating a task in another service does not automatically mean all future changes synchronize both ways.
