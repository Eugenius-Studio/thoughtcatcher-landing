Feature guide

Capture tasks in the language you think in

Use Blurts for multilingual task capture, understand language preservation, and review names, dates, and mixed-language recordings carefully.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

Keep the thought in your own words

Translating a fleeting thought before recording it adds another step at the moment you are trying to remember it. Blurts uses automatic language detection during transcription and asks its task extraction system to preserve the language of the input. The intended result is a useful task in the language you spoke, rather than a compulsory English rewrite.

That is a product behavior, not a promise that every accent, phrase, or language combination will be recognized perfectly. Speech recognition, task interpretation, and the interface language are separate matters. A task title can remain in Spanish, for example, while the app’s field labels continue to display their existing interface text.

How to start

Open the recording control and speak a short set of actions in the language you normally use for those tasks. The capture flow does not require a language picker before recording. Stop the recording, allow online processing to finish, and inspect the task titles and descriptions. Correct any name or instruction whose spelling or meaning is important.

Use a complete action when possible. A brief phrase can be enough, but a person’s name or a project nickname alone may not communicate what you intend to do. For a first test, choose familiar everyday actions and verify the result before relying on the same pattern for important commitments.

  • Check the action, not just whether the words look fluent.
  • Review personal names, place names, product names, and abbreviations.
  • Verify a date separately when you use a relative expression.

Working with more than one language

Many people switch languages within a sentence or use English project terms while speaking another language. You can try that natural style, but the feature does not establish a guarantee of perfect code-switching or word-for-word preservation of every language boundary. Treat a mixed-language recording as something to verify with your own examples.

For example, you might describe a family errand in Spanish and then refer to a work document by its English title. Inspect whether those became the correct separate tasks and whether the document name survived intact. If the combined recording repeatedly produces an incorrect title, a shorter recording or a quick title edit may be the more reliable workflow.

Understand the limits of the result

The task output is a concise interpretation, not a certified translation or a verbatim transcript. A fluent-looking sentence can still omit an important condition. Check phrases such as “after they confirm” or “ask whether,” because changing them can change the commitment. Do not infer that a natural-language feature understands every cultural or regional date convention without review.

Live words shown during recording are also separate from the final processing. The live preview depends on speech support on your device, while the final task extraction uses the processed recording. If the preview looks incomplete, wait for the actual result before deciding that the capture failed. If the final meaning is wrong, edit the task or retry with clearer wording.

Common questions

Do I need to select a language first?

The reviewed recording flow uses automatic language detection and has no required language-selection step.

Will Blurts translate all my tasks into English?

The extraction instructions ask for titles and descriptions in the input language. Review the result, especially with mixed-language speech.

Is there a verified number of supported languages?

This guide does not claim a tested language count. Try your own language, accent, and common task vocabulary, and check the output before depending on it.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
