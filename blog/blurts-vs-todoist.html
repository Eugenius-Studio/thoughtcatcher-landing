Blurts vs Todoist: choosing a voice capture workflow

Compare Blurts with Todoist and Ramble for spoken task capture, daily planning, existing workflows, and the real cost of switching.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 4 min read
Decision Blurts Todoist

Where work lives Blurts plus supported destinations Projects, lists, boards and planning in Todoist
Connection between them No verified Todoist connector Manual transfer would add a step

The choice starts with where you finish your work

If you already organize your day in Todoist, try its native Ramble feature before adding another capture app. Ramble turns spoken thoughts into structured tasks, so voice capture alone is not a reason to switch. Blurts is worth evaluating when your main difficulty is getting a scattered set of intentions out of your head and into usable tasks on your phone.

The useful comparison is the complete journey: start recording, express several intentions, review the result, and find the tasks when it is time to act. A pleasant recording screen is only part of that journey. The better choice leaves less correction and less searching afterward. This article compares documented capabilities and offers a practical evaluation; it does not claim a measured speed or accuracy winner.

Todoist Ramble is a direct alternative

Todoist documents Ramble as speech-to-task capture that recognizes multiple actions and attributes such as dates, priorities, and durations. Its current instructions cover project and label selection, review before adding, and ways to launch capture on mobile. It requires an internet connection. Treat comparisons that describe Todoist as keyboard-only as outdated.

Todoist’s plans combine capture with a larger task-management system: projects, list and board layouts, reminders, filters, and additional planning features on paid tiers. Ramble has limited sessions on the free plan and unlimited sessions on current Pro and Business plans, subject to the vendor’s stated conditions. That combination can be compelling when Todoist already contains your commitments.

Where Blurts belongs in the decision

Blurts focuses on speaking a ramble and turning it into clean tasks. That is a useful starting point for someone who avoids typing small commitments into a planner. However, a focused capture experience must earn its place beside a tool that already performs the same broad function. The test is whether you capture more of the tasks you actually mean to keep, with less correction and less effort afterward.

Blurts can send captured tasks to Apple Reminders, Notion, Google Tasks, or Microsoft Planner, so it is most relevant when one of those is already your destination. It also keeps an editable task list with date groups and Lens filters. There is no direct Todoist connector: using Blurts as an input to Todoist would require an additional transfer step. If your work already lives in Todoist, that is a meaningful disadvantage to weigh against any preference for Blurts capture. Compare the supported destinations and current Blurts plans before adding a subscription.

Try a realistic mixed-task example

Imagine you have just finished a project discussion and also remembered two personal errands. Your test input might be: “Send Maya the revised outline tomorrow, check whether the venue needs a deposit, and buy coffee filters.” Use your own ordinary speech, including a pause or self-correction. Do not rehearse a command designed to flatter either product.

Then inspect the result. Are there three actionable tasks? Did “check whether” remain a check rather than become an instruction to pay? Is tomorrow attached to the outline rather than every task? Can you put the personal item somewhere you will see it outside work? These are evaluation questions, not a claim that either product will always get the example right.

  • Count missing tasks, unwanted tasks, and details you must correct.
  • Include the time spent moving or reorganizing the results.
  • Repeat with a short reminder, a longer ramble, and a correction halfway through.

Compare the cost of keeping your current system

A subscription comparison should begin with what you already pay for. If your Todoist plan already includes the amount of Ramble you need, a separate capture subscription is an additional expense. If you only need occasional capture, the free allowance may be sufficient. Check current billing terms and your account’s plan rather than assuming a headline price applies to you.

Also count setup and review. A different app can be worthwhile if it removes repeated work, but moving projects, recreating filters, or maintaining two inboxes can absorb the gain. Do not migrate a carefully maintained system on the strength of one successful recording. Try several ordinary days while keeping your current task list intact.

When to keep Todoist, and when to test Blurts

Keep Todoist as your primary system when its projects, shared work, and planning views are where you decide what to do next. Test Blurts when the capture moment is still failing despite having a planner: ideas stay in your head, voice memos pile up, or typing causes you to postpone recording a commitment. Decide on the tasks you successfully recover later, not the size of the feature list.

Using both makes sense only if the handoff is explicit. Confirm a currently supported connection before assuming tasks can move automatically between them. Without one, include manual transfer in the trial. A workflow that feels effortless during capture but leaves tasks in an unattended second inbox has not solved the original problem.

Common questions

Does Todoist support voice capture?

Yes. Ramble captures spoken tasks and interprets task details. It is a meaningful native alternative to a separate voice capture app.

Is Blurts more accurate than Todoist Ramble?

There is no comparative benchmark presented here. Test both with your language, typical wording, microphone, and actual tasks, then compare the corrections required.

Do I need to replace Todoist to try Blurts?

No. Keep your established system during the trial. Verify any supported integration and judge the entire capture-to-action workflow before changing where your tasks live.
