Blurts vs Google Tasks: capture thoughts without losing your Google workflow

Compare Blurts with Google Tasks and Gemini for task capture, Gmail and Calendar workflows, account requirements, and everyday review.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 4 min read
Decision Blurts Google Tasks
Capture Voice recording to several saved tasks Gemini offers a native conversational route
Connection Choose a Google Tasks list Tasks remain in the Google workflow
Calendar distinction Creates Google Tasks, not calendar events Dated tasks can be managed in Google Calendar

Your existing Google routine is part of the comparison

Google Tasks is worth keeping when Gmail and Calendar are already where you notice and finish commitments. Blurts is worth testing when the missing step is capturing thoughts that arrive away from those screens. The decision should account for both moments: getting an intention recorded and encountering it again when you can act.

Google Tasks also has a conversational capture route through Gemini. A comparison that treats it as manual typing alone misses an important alternative. Before adding a separate app, try the capabilities available on your actual account. Then compare the result with Blurts using the same everyday tasks and the same standard for what counts as complete.

What Google Tasks and Gemini already provide

Google’s Tasks overview documents synced tasks, details, subtasks, dates, notifications, and entry points from Google products including Gmail and Calendar. This matters when an obligation originates in an email: keeping it close to the original work can reduce the amount of context you must reconstruct later.

Google also documents creating and managing Tasks through Gemini, including requests based on a conversation. The connection has requirements such as the appropriate connected app and activity settings. Gemini Live provides a voice conversation route, with availability and connected-app behavior depending on the user’s setup. Check those requirements before assuming access.

What to expect from a Blurts trial

Blurts centers on turning one spoken ramble into clean tasks. That makes it relevant when you do not begin with an email or a calendar entry: a thought arrives during a break, after a conversation, or while you are preparing to leave. Its usefulness depends on whether that short capture moment reliably produces tasks you can find and act on later.

Blurts can connect to a chosen Google Tasks list after Google sign-in. Captured tasks are saved in Blurts and sent to that list; later Blurts edits and completion changes can be sent outward too. This is not a promise that changes made in Google Tasks return automatically to Blurts. Google Tasks due dates also have different time semantics from a calendar event. Read the Google Tasks setup guide before testing the handoff. This makes coexistence a practical option: compare Blurts plus Google Tasks with Gemini plus Google Tasks, keeping the same destination.

Use two different starting points

First test a task that begins in Gmail, such as replying to a supplier after reviewing an attachment. Evaluate the path from the original message to a visible task and back to the information you need. A spoken summary may be unnecessary if the existing Google workflow already preserves the right context with little effort.

Then test a task that begins only in your head. For example, you need to compare two travel dates, ask a colleague about coverage, and send an update after they reply. Say that naturally. Check whether the output keeps the dependency clear: sending the update is conditional on getting an answer, rather than an instruction to send one immediately. This is an illustrative test, not a report of either product’s measured output.

  • Open the destination list and verify that the tasks exist.
  • Check dates, conditions, and distinctions between personal and work accounts.
  • Count any copy-and-paste work needed to preserve useful context.

Account setup can decide the result

A Google account used for personal life and a Google account used for work are different destinations. Before evaluating convenience, confirm that the capture route writes to the account you actually review. A successful assistant response is not enough if the task is sitting in a personal list while your workday happens in another account.

Apply the same standard to Blurts. Verify the supported connection and where a new task appears. Do not infer that connecting one Google service grants access to every other service. If you cannot complete the handoff with the account and permissions you have, that is a real limitation of your proposed workflow, regardless of how well the recording itself works.

Compare incremental cost and ongoing attention

Start with the Google tools you already use and the features available to your account. Do not assume that a broader paid Google AI plan is required for every Tasks workflow, or that every account has identical Gemini functionality. Consult the current account and product documentation. For Blurts, check the current subscription offer and supported usage before committing.

The recurring cost also includes attention. Maintaining two inboxes, correcting the same date twice, or wondering which system contains a promise can cost more than the initial capture saved. Keep one clear review routine during the trial. Choose the option that reliably preserves the commitments that matter while requiring the least extra maintenance in your actual day.

Common questions

Can Gemini create Google Tasks?

Yes. Google documents creating, updating, and viewing Tasks through Gemini, subject to connection and account requirements. This is part of the comparison, not an unsupported workaround.

Does Blurts replace Google Calendar?

This article evaluates task capture. Do not assume that a voice capture app replaces calendar scheduling or every Google Tasks feature; check the specific supported capabilities.

What is the most useful trial for an existing Google Tasks user?

Keep your current lists. Compare a task originating in Gmail with a set of thoughts captured away from your desk, then inspect where every resulting task appears and how much cleanup it needs.
