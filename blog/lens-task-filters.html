Feature guide

Use Lens to focus your Blurts task list

Filter Blurts tasks by area, switch between priority and time order, and bring waiting items into view without moving the underlying tasks.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

Look at the part of your list that matters now

Lens changes the view of your Blurts tasks without moving or refiling them. You can focus on one area, change the order within each day, or bring things you are waiting on into the main view. It is useful when you want to examine a particular part of your life without creating a separate task system for it.

For example, you may want to review work commitments before leaving your desk, then see family tasks when planning the evening. The tasks keep their original details and dates. Lens supplies a temporary perspective on the same list, so changing a filter does not change the actual commitment.

Open and apply the Lens

Once you have tasks in your list, open the Lens control. Choose an order and, if useful, one of the available areas. The sheet shows area choices represented in your tasks. Use the waiting-on toggle when you want those items visible alongside other work, then tap Done to apply the changes.

Changes in the sheet are a working selection until you tap Done. Reset returns the selection to the default view. If you dismiss the sheet without applying it, do not expect the task list behind it to adopt an unfinished selection. This makes it possible to explore a view without reorganizing your list.

  • Choose Everything to remove an area filter.
  • Choose What matters for priority-led ordering.
  • Choose When it’s due for time-led ordering within each date group.

Understand what each order means

What matters puts higher-priority tasks ahead of lower-priority ones, then considers timing and capture order. When it’s due brings timed tasks into chronological order, followed by untimed work. Neither mode changes a task’s due date or priority; it changes the sequence in which you encounter the items.

The area filter narrows the list to Business, Health, Work, or Family, depending on what is present. It is not a full-text search and does not introduce custom saved filters. If a task is assigned to the wrong area, edit the task itself rather than looking for a Lens setting that can correct its underlying classification.

A practical review before your next block of work

Suppose you have a timed appointment, a high-priority follow-up, a shopping errand, and a reply you are waiting for. Start with Everything to see the whole picture. Then filter to Work and choose the order that matches your immediate decision: importance when deciding what to prioritize, or timing when checking the sequence of the day.

Turn on the waiting-on view when you want to review dependencies. A waiting task remains something dependent on another person; showing it beside your own actions does not mean the dependency is resolved. Use the resulting view to decide whether you need a separate follow-up task or simply need to keep waiting.

Why a filter can disappear

Lens is a temporary view. Its state is not stored as a permanent preference or synchronized as a shared view. It resets on a fresh app start and when the app checks it after the local day changes. This helps prevent yesterday’s narrow view from hiding today’s commitments without you noticing.

If you think a task has vanished, return to Everything or use Reset, then check the date groups and folded sections. A filter does not delete tasks. If the control is absent on an empty list, capture a task first; Lens is useful once there is something to narrow or reorder.

Common questions

Does Lens move tasks between lists?

No. It changes the presentation of the same tasks. Editing a task’s fields is a separate action.

Can I save a permanent Lens view?

The current Lens is session-based and resets across a fresh start or day change. It is not a library of saved filters.

Why is one area missing from the choices?

The sheet shows the areas represented in the current task counts. Check the task’s Area field if you expected it to appear under another area.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
