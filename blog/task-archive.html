Find older Blurts tasks in the archive

Understand Blurts’ automatic archive, how older pending tasks are selected, and how to review them without confusing archive with completion.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 3 min read

An older task can move without being completed

Blurts includes an archive section for older pending tasks. The current automatic rule uses the age of the capture: a pending task captured more than seven days ago can be archived during a list refresh. Archiving does not mean you completed the work, and it is different from deliberately deleting a task.

This behavior is worth understanding before relying on the visible daily list alone. The age rule does not exclude a task because it has a future due date. A commitment recorded well in advance can therefore appear in the archive when you return later. Use the archive as part of your review when looking for an older item.

Open and inspect archived work

Open your task list and expand the archive section when it contains items. Review the titles and details just as you would any other task. If you cannot find a previously captured commitment in the usual date groups, check here before recording it again. A second capture can create another record without resolving what happened to the first.

You can inspect an archived task’s context and use its available task controls. Treat a title as a reminder of the intention you recorded at that time, then decide whether it is still relevant. Do not mark an unfinished task complete merely to make the archive look tidy.

  • Check the archive when an older task is absent from the active list.
  • Distinguish no longer relevant from actually completed.
  • Verify the task’s date if it was captured far ahead of time.

A concrete example

Suppose you record “Confirm the workshop equipment in three weeks.” You may not need to act immediately, but the task has already entered the system. If it remains pending beyond the archive threshold, its capture age can move it into the archive even though the due date is still ahead.

That example explains why an archive review should be part of checking future commitments. The archive is not a completed-work history and should not be read as a list of obligations you intentionally dismissed. The current rule is automatic; it does not ask whether each older commitment still matters to you.

How this affects widgets and integrations

The home-screen task snapshot excludes archived tasks, so an older item can also disappear from that small view. Open the full app when checking whether all commitments are accounted for. A widget showing a few upcoming items cannot substitute for reviewing the archive when something seems missing.

Automatic Blurts archiving should also be distinguished from deletion handling for a linked Notion page or Google task. The archive operation changes the Blurts task record. This guide does not claim that it automatically archives, completes, or deletes every linked item in every external destination.

Review without creating extra maintenance

Use an archive check to recover context and decide what still deserves attention. If a task is finished, record that honestly. If it no longer represents a commitment, use the appropriate delete control after checking what connected copies may be affected. If its date or wording is wrong, edit it rather than creating a near-duplicate.

For an important future deadline, keep a review routine that includes older captured tasks. The archive behavior is a constraint to account for, not a reason to assume the system will remind you of every future obligation in the active list. If an item remains difficult to locate, inspect the active Lens and folded sections as well.

Common questions

Does archived mean completed?

No. The automatic archive specifically selects older pending tasks. Completion is a separate status.

Does a future date prevent archiving?

The current rule uses capture age and does not exempt a future due date.

Does the widget include archived items?

No. Its snapshot excludes archived tasks. Review the archive inside the app when looking for older work.

Try one real thought.

Start with something you actually need to do. Review the result and decide whether the workflow fits your day.
