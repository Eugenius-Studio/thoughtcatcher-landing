Blurts vs Apple Reminders and Siri: when built-in capture is enough

Compare Blurts with Apple Reminders and Siri for everyday reminders, spoken task capture, shared lists, and a sensible way to test an alternative.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 4 min read
Decision Blurts Apple Reminders
Capture Record a ramble and review extracted tasks Siri, dictation and supported Apple Intelligence features
Ecosystem Mobile app on iPhone and Android Built into the Apple ecosystem
Connection between them Direct Reminders export is not verified Keep existing lists while testing a separate workflow

Begin with the reminder you actually need

If your goal is to remember one clear action at a known time, Apple Reminders and Siri deserve to be your starting point. They already fit many iPhone routines. Blurts becomes interesting when the difficult part comes earlier: you have a jumble of intentions and want to turn it into clean tasks before deciding what happens next.

The distinction is about your situation, not whether one app can use a microphone. Apple supports speaking and dictating reminders. A fair comparison asks how well each approach handles the particular things you forget: a single errand, a set of unrelated follow-ups, a shared household list, or an idea that still needs clarification.

What Apple Reminders already does

Apple’s getting-started guide documents reminders created by typing, dictation, or Siri, along with grocery lists, shared lists, tags, and Smart Lists. Its reminder creation guide also describes Apple Intelligence assistance for descriptions and suggested reminders. Availability depends on compatible devices, software, language, region, and account setup.

These are meaningful strengths for people whose commitments already live across Apple devices. Apple also notes that the full set of described Reminders features depends on updated iCloud reminders; other account types may differ. Do not treat the experience on one person’s iPhone as proof that every Apple account and device behaves identically.

Where a separate capture app may help

Blurts focuses on one spoken ramble becoming clean tasks. That may suit the moment when you know several things need doing but do not want to stop and formulate each as a separate entry. The value to look for is not merely a transcript. It is a set of tasks whose wording still matches your intentions and which you can review without replaying the whole recording.

Blurts records up to two minutes of speech, extracts separate tasks, and saves them before showing the results. It offers date groups, editable fields, and Lens filters, with connected destinations in Notion, Google Tasks, and Microsoft Planner. The Apple option in settings signs you in to Blurts; it does not itself connect a Reminders list. Direct Reminders delivery has not been verified here. Blurts’ daily reminder is a morning review prompt, not a substitute for a time-specific or location-based reminder you already rely on.

Compare a clear reminder with a messy set of intentions

Use two examples. First, capture a simple reminder to put a library book in your bag tomorrow morning. Then try a more natural stream: the book needs returning, the apartment inspection needs confirming, and you should ask a friend which restaurant they booked. Include a correction if you normally change your mind while speaking.

Review the outputs for different reasons. In the first test, the right timing and a dependable place to see the reminder are central. In the second, you care about separating actions and keeping uncertainties intact. “Ask which restaurant” must not become “book a restaurant.” Neither this scenario nor a screenshot establishes that either app will interpret every sentence correctly; it is a way to test your own needs.

  • Try the exact Apple device, account, and Siri language you use each day.
  • Check whether household tasks remain visible to the people who need them.
  • Verify that timing and task wording survive any handoff between apps.

Account for familiarity before paying for another tool

The built-in workflow has a practical advantage when you already know how to use it. You do not have to teach yourself a new place to look each morning. A separate app should repay the effort of installing it, learning its review flow, and remembering where tasks are stored. That trade can be worthwhile, but it should be visible during an ordinary week.

Compare the incremental cost you would actually incur. Check Blurts’ current offer before subscribing, and consider whether your existing Apple setup already performs the necessary work. Avoid paying for a different recording interface if the real problem is that you never review your task list. Equally, do not dismiss a focused app if easier capture reliably gets previously forgotten commitments onto that list.

Make a small trial before moving your lists

Keep shared groceries, recurring chores, and established reminders where they already work. Try Blurts with a bounded set of new personal tasks. At the end of each day, check whether you can find and act on every captured commitment. This exposes the cost of a second inbox before you move anything important.

If your intended workflow is to capture in Blurts and review in Apple Reminders, verify that exact integration and its behavior in the current app. Do not infer automatic synchronization from an Apple logo or general integration language. A clear handoff lets you improve capture while preserving a routine that works. An unclear handoff is a reason to keep the simpler setup until you have tested it.

Common questions

Can Siri already create reminders by voice?

Yes. Apple documents Siri and dictation as ways to create reminders. Blurts should be evaluated against that real baseline, not against typing alone.

Does this comparison assume every iPhone has the same AI features?

No. Apple Intelligence features have device, software, language, and regional requirements. Check Apple’s current documentation for your setup.

Should I move all my reminders to Blurts?

Start with a small trial. Preserve shared lists and recurring reminders until you have verified the features and handoff you need, and confirmed that the new workflow reduces effort.

Does the Apple option in Blurts settings connect Reminders?

That option runs Sign in with Apple to identify your Blurts account. It does not itself request Reminders access or send tasks to a Reminders list.
