Blurts vs TickTick: voice capture or a complete daily planner?

An honest comparison of Blurts and TickTick, including AI Voice Add, calendar planning, habits, review effort, and when keeping both makes sense.

By the Blurts team November 9, 2026 4 min read
Decision Blurts TickTick
Capture Short voice capture with task review AI Voice Add also splits multiple spoken tasks
Daily workflow Focused list and connected destinations Tasks, calendar planning, habits and focus tools
Connection between them No verified TickTick connector Evaluate native capture before adding a second inbox

Start with the part of your day that needs help

Choose a daily planner because it helps you decide and do the work. Choose a capture tool because it helps you preserve intentions before they disappear. TickTick addresses a broad daily-planning workflow, while Blurts centers on turning spoken thoughts into tasks. There is overlap: TickTick now offers AI voice capture that can split a single input into multiple tasks.

That overlap matters. If you already use TickTick successfully, adding Blurts needs a more specific reason than wanting to speak instead of type. The question is whether a separate capture experience improves the moments when you currently lose tasks, without creating another place to maintain. Compare the work remaining after capture as carefully as the recording itself.

TickTick’s voice features go beyond basic dictation

TickTick’s official product update describes AI Voice Add on mobile. It can identify task dates, lists, tags, and priorities, and split multiple tasks from one spoken input. The same update documents an AI assistant for task creation and organization, as well as transcription and summaries for audio attachments. It would be inaccurate to describe TickTick as a text-only alternative.

The feature overview also covers lists, tags, filters, reminders, calendar views, focus tools, and habits. These features serve different moments: capturing an intention, arranging a day, starting a focused session, and maintaining a routine. Their value depends on whether you use those moments in one place.

What a focused Blurts workflow should improve

Blurts is designed around the initial handoff from a spoken ramble to clean tasks. For a person who remembers obligations while changing context, that can be the part worth optimizing. The meaningful benefit is fewer intentions left unrecorded and less cleanup before the tasks are usable. That is something to verify in your own routine, rather than assume from a narrower feature set.

Blurts gives a captured task a title, optional description, type, area, priority, and date when one is mentioned. You can correct these afterward, use date groups to review the list, and narrow it with Lens. Its connected destinations are Notion, Google Tasks, and Microsoft Planner. A direct TickTick connection is not supported here, so maintaining TickTick as your main list would add a transfer step. Choose Blurts for its capture and review workflow only if that complete path works better for you; it does not replace TickTick’s habit and focus tools.

A practical test: planning tomorrow without losing today’s thoughts

Picture an evening review. You remember a work follow-up, a shopping item, and a recurring household chore. Then you change your mind about when the work item should happen. Speak the same natural input into each app. Review whether the output preserves the correction, separates the three actions, and leaves the household routine in a form you can maintain.

Next, continue into your real planning process. Place the work in tomorrow’s plan and find the shopping item from your phone. If you normally use a timer or a habit tracker, keep those steps in the evaluation. A capture app does not automatically replace all the routines surrounding a task, and a planner’s breadth does not automatically make its capture experience right for you.

  • Check whether separate actions became separate tasks.
  • Check the meaning of dates and any repeated schedule before trusting the result.
  • Measure the complete review and organization effort, including any transfer between apps.

Pricing is partly about the features you already use

TickTick offers free and Premium plans. Its current plan comparison places AI features and expanded planning capabilities in the paid offering and distinguishes limits across lists, tasks, reminders, and other tools. Verify the entitlement on your platform and account before making AI capture the basis of a purchase. This comparison deliberately avoids a fixed price that could vary by store or billing period.

If you already pay for TickTick’s calendar or other planning tools, evaluate its included capture experience first. Blurts would need to justify an additional workflow or expense through an observable improvement. If you do not need TickTick’s broader planning features, compare the cost and effort of the smaller workflow you actually intend to use.

Keep one clear place to review commitments

TickTick is the stronger fit when your decision depends on keeping tasks, calendar planning, habits, and focus routines together. Blurts is a candidate when getting thoughts into tasks is the stubborn gap in an otherwise workable routine. Neither choice removes the need to decide which tasks matter or when you will do them.

Coexistence can work, but only after you define the handoff. Do not assume Blurts connects to TickTick because both products support integrations generally. Check the exact supported destination and transfer behavior. If a task must be copied manually, include that work in the trial. Keep the place where you review commitments unambiguous so a new capture habit does not fragment your day.

Common questions

Can TickTick split one voice input into several tasks?

Yes. Its official AI Voice Add documentation describes automatic splitting of multiple tasks on mobile, along with recognition of task details.

Does Blurts replace TickTick’s calendar and habit features?

Do not assume feature parity. Evaluate Blurts for its verified capture workflow and keep the planning tools you rely on unless their replacements are explicitly supported.

Which one is better for a quick brain dump?

Use your own examples to decide. Compare omissions, incorrect details, review effort, and where the resulting tasks end up; this article does not report a head-to-head performance test.
